# Consciousness

**Consciousness** refers to the subjective, first-person experience of being aware and having mental states. It encompasses both the content of experience (what we're conscious of) and the fact that there's something it's like to have experiences.

## Key Distinctions

### Phenomenal vs Access Consciousness

- **Phenomenal Consciousness**: The experiential, qualitative aspects of mental states[^1]
- **Access Consciousness**: Information available for reasoning, reporting, and controlling behavior

### Levels of Consciousness

1. **Full Consciousness**: Normal waking awareness
2. **Altered States**: Sleep, anesthesia, meditation
3. **Unconscious Processing**: Information processing without awareness

## Neural Correlates

The search for [[Neural Correlates of Consciousness]] focuses on identifying brain activity patterns associated with conscious experience.

Key brain networks:
- **Default Mode Network**: Active during rest and self-referential thinking
- **Global Workspace**: Integration of information across brain regions
- **Thalamic Circuits**: Regulation of consciousness levels

## Measurement Challenges

Studying consciousness presents unique methodological challenges:

- **Subjective Reports**: Reliance on first-person accounts
- **Access vs Experience**: Difficulty distinguishing what's conscious from what's reportable
- **Individual Differences**: Variation in conscious experience across people

## Theoretical Frameworks

### Global Workspace Theory
Information becomes conscious when it's globally available across brain systems.

### Integrated Information Theory
Consciousness corresponds to integrated information (Φ) in a system.

### Predictive Processing
Consciousness arises from the brain's predictive models of sensory input.

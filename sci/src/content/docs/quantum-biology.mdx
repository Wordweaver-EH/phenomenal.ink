# Quantum Coherence in Biological Systems

For decades, the prevailing wisdom was that quantum effects could not survive in the "warm, wet, and noisy" environment of the cell. However, recent evidence suggests that life may have found a way to harness these delicate phenomena.

## Photosynthetic Efficiency

The most famous example comes from photosynthesis. When a photon hits a light-harvesting complex, the excitation energy must find its way to the reaction center. Classical random walk models fail to explain the near-perfect efficiency observed in nature.

Studies of the Fenna-Matthews-Olson (FMO) complex suggest that the energy travels in a superposition of paths, effectively "sampling" all routes simultaneously to find the most efficient one. This quantum coherence, usually fragile, appears to be protected by the protein scaffold's vibrational modes.

## Magnetoreception

Another frontier is avian magnetoreception. The radical pair mechanism proposes that the magnetic compass of birds relies on quantum entangled electron pairs created in cryptochrome proteins in the eye.
